Abstract

Background: Effective communication at hospital discharge is important for continuity of care. Discharge summaries can include requests for follow-up actions in primary care, but these requests are difficult to study at scale because they are often written in free text.

Objective: To describe temporal patterns in GP-directed requests documented in discharge summaries from one NHS hospital trust.

Methods: We used large language model extraction to identify requests in approximately 260,000 discharge summaries from Salford Royal Hospital between 2014 and 2023. Extracted requests were classified by request type and stated timeframe.

Results: The average number of extracted GP-directed requests per discharge was 52% higher at the end of the study period than at the beginning. Extracted requested timeframes also increased, and the composition of request categories changed over time.

Interpretation: These findings describe changes in documented discharge requests. They should not be interpreted as direct measures of workload, task completion, patient outcomes, or causal change in service delivery.

Methods

1. Source data

Discharge summaries from Salford Royal Hospital covering 2014–2023.

2. Extraction

A large language model was prompted to identify requests directed to GPs or primary care teams.

3. Classification

Requests were grouped by type and, where present, stated timeframe before temporal summaries were calculated.

Limitations

This is a descriptive analysis of requests documented in discharge summaries from a single hospital trust. Observed patterns may reflect changes in clinical need, service configuration, documentation conventions, model extraction performance, or a combination of these factors.

The study does not directly measure whether requested actions were completed, how much time they required, whether they were clinically appropriate, or whether they affected patient outcomes.
